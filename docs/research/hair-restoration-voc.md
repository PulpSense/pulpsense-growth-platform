# US Hair-Restoration Practice VOC — Funnel Research Brief

**Audience:** US clinic owners/surgeons, practice managers, patient/treatment coordinators, and marketing leads
**Purpose:** Sharpen PulpSense’s high-CPL Meta funnel
**Method:** Rapid desk study of trade surveys, clinical guidance, a financing source, and patient forum discussions. Patient posts are directional VOC, not a representative survey. **Observed evidence** and **commercial inference** are labeled separately.

## Executive synthesis

The clinic is not buying “more leads.” It is buying **more consults with viable donor hair, realistic expectations, enough budget, and enough trust to choose a surgeon rather than keep shopping**. The core leak is therefore not one number called CPL; it is the chain from inquiry → contact → qualified consult → show → treatment plan → financed/booked case.

The generic promise **“45 more hair-restoration calls in 90 days”** is volume-first and can activate the buyer’s fear of low-intent price shoppers. Reframe around a **measurable consult-to-case system**: *“See where qualified hair-restoration demand leaks—from first inquiry to attended consult to booked procedure—and what recovering it is worth.”* Keep “45” only if PulpSense can document the denominator, definition of a call, period, client distribution, and exclusions.

The second promise—**“see who Google or ChatGPT recommends ahead of you”**—works best as a diagnostic wedge, not the whole offer. Tie visibility to the buyer outcome: *“See which local competitors patients encounter while they compare surgeons, proof, price, and financing—and where your practice disappears.”*

## Evidence ledger: what the market actually says

1. **The category is both surgical and recurring/non-surgical.** ISHRS reported 1,592,588 non-surgical patients and 628,604 surgical patients treated in 2021; common non-surgical treatments included finasteride, minoxidil, and PRP.[1] **Implication (high confidence):** segment economics and nurture by treatment path instead of treating every lead as an FUE prospect.
2. **Younger patients and women are meaningful growth segments.** ISHRS says 95% of first-time surgical patients in 2024 initiated surgery at ages 20–35, while female surgical patients increased 16.5% from 2021.[2] **Implication (medium-high):** test younger-life-stage and female-specific creative, but avoid implying everyone in these groups is clinically eligible.
3. **The emotional “why” is appearance and status, not grafts.** ISHRS members reported “become/feel more attractive” as the leading reason (90%), followed by “appear younger to compete in the workplace” (63%).[2] **Implication (high):** patient-facing proof should sell natural, identity-restoring change; buyer-facing copy should promise a pipeline of patients seeking that change.
4. **Outcome quality is surgeon-dependent.** The AAD states, “Results depend largely on the surgeon you select,” while noting modern hairline design and placement can make results look natural.[3] **Implication (high):** surgeon credibility, case selection, and comparable before/after proof should outrank generic technology badges.
5. **The result lag is long and visually volatile.** AAD says most patients see results in six to nine months, some at 12 months, and hair may look thinner by month three.[3] **Implication (high):** build long-cycle nurture and expectation-setting; do not optimize only for same-week bookings.
6. **Qualification is clinical, not merely demographic.** AAD identifies sufficient healthy donor hair and the ability to grow hair in the recipient area as core candidacy requirements.[3] StatPearls adds stable loss pattern, healthy scalp, donor density, and realistic expectations.[4] **Implication (high):** forms should collect stage/area, stability, current treatment, prior procedures, timeline, and willingness to send photos—without diagnosing online.
7. **Price is substantial and usually self-pay.** CareCredit’s July 2026 guide gives a US average range of **$3,919–$10,640**, notes FUE generally costs more than FUT, and says cosmetic hair surgery is not typically covered by insurance.[5] **Implication (medium-high):** financing and monthly-payment readiness belong in the funnel before a coordinator spends heavy follow-up time.
8. **Price shopping is inseparable from medical tourism.** A patient described UK pricing near £10k versus roughly one-third in Turkey, while still seeking “the BEST possible clinic” and a “natural looking ‘mature’” hairline; another replied, “I am not playing dice with MY head!”[6] **Implication (medium):** do not compete on cheapness. Contrast local accountability, surgeon involvement, diagnosis, repair avoidance, follow-up, and conservative donor planning.
9. **Prospects comparison-shop multiple clinical opinions.** One patient consulted four clinics in one week, compared graft estimates and density recommendations, and deliberately withheld prices because “I really just want the best surgery and end result.”[7] **Implication (medium):** make the consultation produce a differentiated, legible plan—not merely a quote.
10. **Decision cycles can span months and include travel budgeting.** A patient asked how early to consult for surgery “next summer” to estimate procedure and travel cost; a respondent said it took “several months to find the surgeon I am comfortable with.”[8] **Implication (medium):** measure assisted conversions over 6–12 months and nurture research-stage leads.
11. **A salesy or shallow consult destroys trust.** In a negative account, a consultant appeared unsure of the diagnosis, gave little examination, and promoted in-house LLLT/shampoo; the patient asked whether this was “a normal experience.”[9] **Implication (medium):** lead with diagnosis, candidacy, limitations, and next steps before products or urgency.
12. **Financing is explicit VOC, not a backend detail.** One patient asked for the “lowest rate” personal loan and “the cheapest way to get the $$$.”[10] **Implication (medium):** show financing availability early, but do not lead with debt or advertise unaudited monthly-payment figures.

## Economics and operating constraints

### Surgical path

- **Observed:** US self-pay ticket is often several thousand dollars; technique, graft count, technology, and surgeon affect price.[5]
- **Observed:** modern surgery may reach the desired result in fewer procedures—68% of surveyed ISHRS members reported an average of one procedure per patient in 2021, versus higher historical estimates.[1]
- **Inference (high):** one incremental booked case can justify significant acquisition spend, but gross revenue is not contribution margin. A calculator must subtract media, sales/coordination labor, financing fees, variable clinical cost, and cancellation/refund risk.
- **Inference (high):** the scarce resource is not a raw phone call; it is surgeon/coordinator capacity spent on candidates who show, can be treated, and can pay.

### Non-surgical path

- **Observed:** non-surgical patient volume exceeded surgical volume in the ISHRS 2021 survey, and includes medication and PRP pathways.[1]
- **Inference (medium):** these leads can create recurring visits, future surgery candidates, and retention value, but their economics differ by protocol and practice. Keep them in a separate forecast rather than assigning surgical case value.

### Consult, lead, and sales friction

| Friction | What it sounds like | Funnel response |
|---|---|---|
| Low clinical fit | “Am I a good candidate?” “How many grafts?” | Photo-ready pre-screen, loss/stability/current-treatment questions, clear “not a diagnosis” language |
| Price shopping | “What did you pay?” “Why does it cost so much?” | Give range logic and financing route; sell planning, safety, and accountability before exact quote |
| Comparison shopping | “I’ve had 4 online consultations” | Deliver a surgeon-led point of view and a written next-step plan |
| Medical tourism | “Same quality…half the price?” | Local continuity, who performs each step, complication/repair plan, follow-up availability |
| Long consideration | “Next summer”; “several months” | 6–12 month nurture, retargeting, case stories by month, reactivation |
| Trust breakdown | unsure diagnosis + product pitch | Consultation standards, credentials, named clinician, limitations, no-pressure promise |
| Financing | “lowest rate”; “cheapest way” | Financing eligibility option and budget band before high-touch coordinator time |
| No-show risk | **Inference; not directly quantified in this study** | calendar reminders, SMS confirmation, deposit policy test, photo/task completion, easy reschedule |
| Agency distrust | **Inference; not directly evidenced in public buyer material here** | expose definitions, source-level attribution, call recordings/dispositions, booked-case reporting—not vanity CPL |

## VOC dictionary

| Theme | Exact/near-exact patient language | Funnel implication |
|---|---|---|
| Naturalness | “natural looking ‘mature’” hairline; “best surgery and end result”[6][7] | Use comparable, well-lit, dated cases; avoid “perfect density” |
| Risk | “I am not playing dice with MY head!”[6] | Make safety, surgeon role, and donor stewardship visible |
| Research | “4 online consultations”; “several months to find the surgeon”[7][8] | Assume the lead is comparing you; nurture rather than hard-close |
| Budget | “how much money I need to set aside”; “lowest rate”[8][10] | Provide range logic and financing pathway |
| Fit | “good candidate”; “excellent donor supply”[7] | Sell assessment quality, not a guaranteed procedure |
| Distrust | “Does this sound like a normal experience?”[9] | Show what a proper consult includes |
| Transformation | “become/feel more attractive”; “appear younger”[2] | Lead with identity and confidence; support with clinical proof |

**Buyer-side language to mirror (inference, high confidence):** *qualified consult, viable candidate, attended consult, show rate, treatment plan, booked case, cost per booked case, coordinator follow-up, financing-ready, source attribution, surgeon capacity, repair case, donor management.* Avoid marketing abstractions such as *omnichannel demand capture*.

## Buyer jobs, triggers, desired outcomes, and proof

### Jobs to be done

1. Fill consult capacity with people the clinic can actually help.
2. Help coordinators prioritize high-intent, clinically plausible, finance-ready prospects.
3. Shorten time-to-contact and prevent comparison shoppers from going cold.
4. Prove which channels create attended consults and booked cases—not just forms/calls.
5. Defend a US practice against cheap overseas alternatives through trust and continuity.
6. Build non-surgical-to-surgical lifetime value without misclassifying every lead.

### Likely purchase triggers (inference)

- Rising Meta CPL with flat attended consults or cases.
- Coordinator overwhelmed by unreachable/price-only inquiries.
- Surgeon schedule gaps, expansion, new location, or added procedure capacity.
- New competitor/chain or overseas clinic dominating comparison research.
- Poor visibility in Google Maps/search/AI recommendations.
- Agency reports leads while the practice sees no revenue linkage.

### Proof PulpSense should bring

- Funnel math from impression → lead → contacted → booked consult → attended → plan → financed/booked case.
- De-identified call/disposition examples showing how “qualified” is defined.
- Median and range, not only best-case outcomes; clear time window and spend.
- Before/after operating metrics: speed-to-lead, show rate, consult-to-case rate, cost per attended consult/booked case.
- Attribution caveats and CRM reconciliation method.
- Vertical-specific creative examples matching naturalness, candidacy, financing, and local-accountability objections.

## Funnel action plan

### Ad-to-page message match

**Recommended control message**

- **Ad hook:** “Your Meta report says ‘leads.’ Your coordinator says ‘price shoppers.’”
- **Primary promise:** “Find the leaks between inquiry, attended consult, and booked hair-restoration case.”
- **Mechanism:** PulpSense maps source + call + disposition + consult outcome, then prioritizes the messages and follow-up that produce viable candidates.
- **Landing hero:** “How many qualified hair-restoration cases are hiding inside the leads you already pay for?”
- **CTA:** **Get My Consult-to-Case Leak Map** (better matched than “Book a demo”).

**Visibility wedge:** “See who Google and ChatGPT surface while patients compare surgeons—and whether your proof answers the questions that make them book.” Do not imply authoritative or exhaustive access to ChatGPT recommendation rankings.

### Recommended page sections

1. **Hero:** qualified cases, not call volume.
2. **Recognition strip:** “Unreachable / price-only / not a candidate / no-show / still researching / chose Turkey / booked elsewhere.”
3. **Leak map:** inquiry → contact → photo/pre-screen → consult booked → attended → plan → finance → case.
4. **Economics calculator:** surgical and non-surgical paths separated.
5. **What qualified means:** practice-configurable clinical, geographic, timing, and financial gates.
6. **Comparison journey:** Google/Maps/AI/community research and competitor proof audit.
7. **Trust-proof framework:** surgeon involvement, credential verification, comparable cases, follow-up, repair/accountability.
8. **Operational proof:** call dispositions, speed-to-lead, show-rate workflows, CRM/revenue reconciliation.
9. **Case study:** denominator, timeframe, spend, median/range, qualification definition.
10. **CTA + form:** request the leak map; set expectation for a 20-minute working session.

### Calculator ideas

**1) Surgical lost-case value**
`Monthly leads × contact rate × qualified rate × consult-book rate × show rate × case-close rate × contribution per case`
Show current vs. improved scenario. Default fields blank; do not seed a universal margin.

**2) Coordinator waste**
`Unqualified/unreachable leads × average follow-up minutes × loaded hourly cost`
Translate “cheap CPL” into lost staff capacity.

**3) No-show recovery**
`Booked consults × current no-show rate × recoverable share × case-close rate × contribution per case`
Label recoverable share as a user assumption, not a PulpSense benchmark.

**4) Mixed-path LTV**
Separate surgery, PRP/other procedures, and medical therapy; include visits/year, retention, and conversion-to-surgery assumptions. Never apply the surgical ticket to all leads.

### CTA and form questions

**Primary CTA:** Get My Consult-to-Case Leak Map
**Secondary CTA:** See Who Patients Find Before Your Practice

Ask only fields that change diagnosis or routing:

1. Role: owner/surgeon, practice manager, coordinator, marketing lead.
2. Locations and service radius.
3. Monthly hair-restoration inquiries by source (ranges).
4. Monthly booked and attended consults.
5. Monthly surgical cases and non-surgical starts (separate).
6. Biggest leak: unreachable, poor fit, price-only, no-show, slow decision, financing, chose competitor/overseas, unknown.
7. Current CRM/call tracking and whether dispositions are recorded.
8. Average surgical revenue **and contribution estimate** (optional ranges, separate).
9. Capacity: how many additional attended consults/cases can the practice serve?
10. Consent to review public search/AI visibility and a sample of de-identified call dispositions.

For the clinic’s downstream patient form, test: location, age band, primary concern/area, duration/stability, current treatment, prior transplant, desired timeline, budget/financing openness, and willingness to send photos. Explicitly say this is not a diagnosis.

### Claims to avoid or qualify

- **“45 more calls in 90 days”** without audited precedent, spend, denominator, and call definition.
- Guaranteed graft count, candidacy, density, permanency for every patient, or result timing.
- “Best surgeon,” “scarless,” “pain-free,” “risk-free,” “no downtime,” or “works for everyone.”
- “ChatGPT ranks you #X” or exhaustive AI recommendation monitoring unless methodology is reproducible.
- Universal ROI based on gross procedure revenue.
- Implied insurance coverage; cosmetic surgery is typically self-pay.[5]
- Fear-based attacks on Turkey/medical tourism. Contrast process, continuity, and accountability instead.
- Cherry-picked before/after imagery without consistent lighting, angle, timeline, and case context.

## Confidence and research gaps

- **High confidence:** clinical qualification, surgeon-dependent trust, natural-result proof, long result lag, self-pay economics, and the need to separate surgical/non-surgical paths are supported by clinical/trade/financing sources.
- **Medium confidence:** comparison-shopping, long decision cycles, financing language, and medical-tourism framing are strongly visible in patient discussions but are not population estimates.
- **Hypotheses requiring PulpSense call/CRM validation:** hair-restoration-specific no-show rate, percent of “bad leads,” coordinator contact attempts, agency distrust prevalence, financing approval friction, and exact consult-to-case benchmarks. Do **not** publish invented industry averages. Validate by coding 100–200 recent leads across source, disposition, show, plan, and revenue outcome.
