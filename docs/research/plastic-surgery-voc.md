# US Plastic-Surgery Practice Buyer VOC

**Purpose:** Funnel-actionable research for PulpSense’s high-CPL Meta funnel.
**Buyer:** US plastic surgeon/practice owner, administrator, patient coordinator, or marketing lead.
**Scope note:** Plastic surgery is the target. Medical-aesthetics evidence is used only where it illuminates the same owner/operator acquisition workflow and is labeled **adjacent**.
**Accessed:** 2026-08-21.

## Executive readout

The buyer does **not** fundamentally want “more leads” or even “more calls.” They want **qualified, finance-ready consultations for the procedures they want to grow, with attribution through surgery booked and revenue collected**. A call-volume promise is weak because it ignores procedure mix, staff capacity, no-shows, consult-to-surgery conversion, financing, and low-fit shoppers.

The sharper promise is a **revenue-leak and competitive-visibility diagnosis**: show where profitable cases are lost between search/AI recommendation, inquiry, first response, consult attendance, financing, and deposit. Lead with visibility and economics; use calls only as a measured intermediate outcome.

**Highest-confidence funnel angle:** “See where high-value surgery cases leak—and which competitors Google and ChatGPT recommend before you.” Avoid guaranteeing case volume. Offer a baseline tied to the practice’s own procedure values and conversion data.

## Buyer language to mirror

Use these phrases because they reflect the economics and source language, without pretending every buyer said them:

- **“Qualified consults,” “surgery-ready,” “finance-ready,” “procedure mix,” “consult-to-close,” “deposit collected,” “case value,” “source-to-surgery attribution.”** *(Inference from evidence above; validate in sales calls.)*
- **“More leads do not always mean better marketing.”**[S12]
- **“Volume over fit,” “no clear accountability,” “content does not sound like us,” “hard-to-exit contract.”**[S12]
- Patient readiness: **“physically, emotionally, and financially” ready.[S11]**
- Patient-side conversion language: **“What will this cost?”, “Who handles my follow-up?”, “Can I trust the reviews?”, “What happens if there is a complication?”, “How long is recovery?”** *(Inference from the factors in S4, S9, and S10; not verbatim survey answers.)*

Avoid condescending terms such as “tire-kickers.” Internally, distinguish **research-stage, timing-misaligned, clinically unsuitable, expectation-misaligned, and financing-constrained** inquiries.

## Buyer pains, trigger events, and desired proof

### Core pains

1. **Lead quality ambiguity:** ads produce inquiries, but the practice cannot see whether they match target procedures, geography, timing, candidacy, or affordability.
2. **Consult leakage:** delayed callbacks, after-hours inquiries, weak handoffs, no self-scheduling, and inconsistent reminders reduce attended consults.[S7][S8]
3. **Economics hidden by vanity metrics:** CPL and calls obscure attended-consult cost, deposit rate, booked-case value, cancellations, refunds, and collected revenue.
4. **Procedure-mix mismatch:** high inquiry volume can fill staff time with lower-priority procedures while desired surgical cases remain flat. *(Inference from S1.)*
5. **Reputation fragility:** reviews reflect staff, scheduling, wait time, billing, communication, and aftercare—not only the result.[S9][S10]
6. **Financing friction:** substantial out-of-pocket cost stops serious considerers.[S5][S6]
7. **Agency distrust:** generic output, opaque work, slow execution, lock-in, and volume without fit.[S12]
8. **Ethical-fit tension:** not every prospect should be closed; expectations and psychological/financial readiness matter.[S11]

### Likely trigger events *(inference; validate on form/calls)*

- New surgeon, location, procedure, device, or OR capacity.
- Recent agency disappointment or contract renewal.
- CPL up while attended consults or surgeries stay flat.
- A visible competitor gaining reviews, local-pack position, or AI recommendations.
- Patient coordinator overwhelmed or response times slipping.
- No-show spike, low financing approvals, or consult-to-deposit decline.
- GLP-1/body-contouring opportunity; ASPS reports 39% of surveyed GLP-1 patients were considering surgery.[S1]
- Reputation shock: negative review, inconsistent review velocity, or poor staff/service feedback.

### Proof this buyer should see

- **Procedure-level funnel:** source → inquiry → first response → booked consult → attended consult → qualified → financing/deposit → surgery booked → collected revenue.
- **Baseline vs. after:** median response time, contact rate, show rate, consult-to-deposit rate, booked-case value, and revenue—not screenshots of lead totals.
- **Competitive evidence:** dated local-pack/search/AI recommendation captures, review gap, procedure-page gap, and citation/source gap. AI outputs must be described as sampled and variable.
- **Operational proof:** example follow-up workflow, missed-call recovery, after-hours handling, reminder/reschedule path, and coordinator ownership.
- **Trust proof:** plastic-surgery-specific examples, references, clear data ownership, implementation timeline, and cancellation/exit terms.
- **Clinical guardrails:** no promise to override surgeon candidacy; qualification supports safety and fit.

## Recommended funnel architecture

1. **Hero — diagnose leakage, not sell calls**
   **“See where high-value plastic-surgery cases leak—and who Google and ChatGPT recommend before you.”**
   Subhead: “Get a procedure-level acquisition audit across visibility, response, consult attendance, financing, and booked surgery.”

2. **Economic reframing**
   “45 calls” can mean 45 wrong-procedure shoppers or a handful of valuable attended consults. Show a simple chain from inquiries to collected case revenue.

3. **Competitive visibility**
   Display a sample market map: local competitors, review volume/recency, procedure coverage, Google position, and sampled AI recommendations. State date, query, location, and variability.

4. **Leak map**
   Five stages: **Found → Contacted → Booked → Attended → Deposited/Booked surgery**. Call out after-hours response, reminders, pricing/financing clarity, and coordinator handoffs.[S4][S7]

5. **Patient trust layer**
   Reviews, surgeon follow-up, transparent cost ranges, staff communication, recovery, and aftercare are acquisition assets.[S4][S9][S10]

6. **Proof**
   Use a procedure-level case study or anonymized dashboard. If PulpSense lacks this proof, label it a **proof gap**; do not substitute generic “calls generated.”

7. **Offer/CTA**
   Primary: **“Map My Case Leakage”**
   Secondary: **“See My Competitive Visibility”**
   Deliverable: a 20-minute diagnostic plus one-page leak/visibility map, not a vague “free strategy call.”

## Calculator concept

### “Booked-case leakage calculator”

Ask for:

- Monthly inquiries by target procedure.
- Average all-in case revenue or deposit value.
- Median minutes/hours to first human response.
- Contact rate.
- Consult booking rate.
- Consult show rate.
- Clinically/financially qualified rate.
- Consult-to-deposit or surgery-booked rate.
- Financing offered and approval rate.

Show:

- **Expected booked cases** = inquiries × contact × booking × show × qualified × close.
- **Expected booked revenue** = expected booked cases × average case revenue.
- **Leak value by stage** = prospects lost at that stage × downstream conversion × average case revenue.
- A sensitivity band, not a guarantee: “If your own show rate moved from X to Y, the modeled difference is Z.”

Never populate “industry-standard” conversion rates without a source and specialty/procedure context. The 70% inquiry-to-consult and consult-to-close target published in an AmSpa vendor article is an aspirational opinion, not a validated plastic-surgery benchmark.[S14]

## CTA and form questions

Keep the first step to 6–8 fields; branch later.

1. Which **2 procedures** do you most want to grow?
2. About how many monthly inquiries do those procedures generate?
3. What is the biggest leak: **not contacted / not booked / no-show / not qualified / financing / no deposit / unknown**?
4. Typical first human response: **<5 min / 5–30 min / 30 min–4 hr / 4–24 hr / >24 hr / unknown**?
5. Do you track leads through **attended consult, deposit, and surgery revenue**?
6. Do you offer patient financing before or during the consult?
7. What changed recently: **new procedure/surgeon/location, agency issue, rising CPL, falling consult quality, coordinator capacity, competitor pressure**?
8. Practice URL + ZIP/market for competitive visibility.

On the booking page, ask one nonjudgmental qualifier: “What would make this diagnostic useful—more qualified consults, fewer leaks, clearer attribution, or competitive visibility?”

## Messaging tests

1. **Calls vs. cases:** “45 more calls” **vs.** “Find the leak between inquiry and booked surgery.”
2. **Competitive visibility:** “See who Google or ChatGPT recommends ahead of you” **vs.** “See why competitors get shortlisted before your practice.”
3. **Procedure specificity:** “Grow your practice” **vs.** “Grow qualified consults for the two procedures you actually want.”
4. **Economics:** “Lower your CPL” **vs.** “Know your cost per attended consult and booked case.”
5. **Lead-quality empathy:** “Get more leads” **vs.** “Stop paying for volume your coordinator cannot convert.”
6. **Reputation:** “Get more reviews” **vs.** “Turn reviews, follow-up, pricing clarity, and aftercare into conversion proof.”
7. **Speed-to-lead:** “Respond instantly” **vs.** “Close the after-hours and callback gaps before buying more traffic.”
8. **Agency distrust:** “Done-for-you marketing” **vs.** “Procedure-level reporting, named accountability, and data you keep.”
9. **Offer:** “Free strategy call” **vs.** “Map My Case Leakage.”
10. **Financing:** “Offer financing” **vs.** “See how many serious consults stall at affordability.”

**Primary success event:** qualified audit bookings from practices with target-procedure volume.
**Downstream quality events:** attended sales call, verified tracking gap, and opportunity accepted—not landing-page CVR alone.

## Claims to avoid

- **“45 more calls in 90 days”** unless the exact definition, baseline, attribution, exclusions, and substantiating client evidence exist. Calls are not qualified consults or booked surgeries.
- **Guaranteed surgeries, revenue, rankings, or AI recommendations.** Search and generative answers vary by query, user, location, and time.
- **“Patients expect a response in five minutes.”** This research found broad-healthcare evidence for a 4–24-hour portal expectation, not a surgery-specific five-minute rule.[S8]
- **Universal conversion benchmarks.** Procedure, market, surgeon, consult fee, candidacy, and financing change the denominator.
- **“Every lead is a potential case.”** Ethical and clinical qualification means some prospects should not advance.[S11]
- **Review manipulation, gating, or implied clinical superiority from star ratings.** A review-site analysis found many platforms do not require appointments and paid packages can affect visibility.[S15]
- **Cheap-price framing.** Use transparent ranges, inclusions, financing, and fit; never minimize surgical risk or pressure financially vulnerable patients.
- **Unqualified HIPAA/compliance claims.** Marketing workflow design is not legal advice.

## Research gaps to close with 5–8 buyer interviews

Ask owners/coordinators for their exact definitions of a “qualified lead,” procedure-level close rates, consult-fee policy, financing approval/deposit flow, after-hours process, agency switching story, and what evidence would justify a second call. This desk study has strong patient-decision and review evidence but limited independent benchmarks for plastic-surgery-specific CPL, speed-to-lead, no-show rate, and consult-to-surgery conversion.
