# US Med-Spa Buyer VOC → Funnel Brief

**Audience:** US med-spa owners/operators, medical directors, practice managers, injectors, and marketing leads
**Purpose:** Make PulpSense’s high-CPL Meta ad → landing page → booked-call path sound like the operator’s business, not a generic lead-gen pitch.
**Research date:** 2026-08-21
**Method:** Rapid synthesis of operator discussions, US consumer research, industry benchmarks, trade data, patient-review language, and federal/clinical guidance. Direct evidence is separated from inference. Search-indexed quotes are labeled and weighted below full-page evidence.

## Executive takeaway

Med-spa buyers do **not** principally need “more calls.” They need more **qualified, locally credible treatment demand that books, shows, buys, and returns**—without discounting their brand or handing another agency money for untraceable “leads.” The economics support this reframing: Zenoti’s 2025 North American platform data puts median revenue/location at **$1.86M** and median ticket at **$216**, but new-guest visits fell **11%**, memberships rose **13%**, and first rebooks cancelled at **37%** versus **4%** for guests rebooked at least twice.[S3] This is a conversion-and-retention system problem, not a raw-call-count problem.

**Recommended core promise:**

> **Turn local aesthetic demand into qualified consults that show—and a follow-up system that brings the right patients back.**

Use “45 more calls in 90 days” only as a subordinate, explicitly evidenced result. “See who Google or ChatGPT recommends ahead of you” is a strong **diagnostic hook**, but the landing page must connect visibility to treatment-specific proof, booked consults, show rate, revenue, and rebooking—not rankings for their own sake.

## Buyer economics and operating reality

### Revenue, treatment mix, repeat behavior

- **Injectables create repeat cadence; devices/skin resurfacing create higher tickets and courses.** Evidence: toxin volume and fee benchmarks versus resurfacing.[S12][S13] **Inference (high):** a med spa should value a lead by treatment pathway and 12-month collected revenue, not first appointment alone.
- **Memberships and staged treatment plans reduce volatility.** Evidence: memberships +13% while both new and existing visits declined.[S3] **Inference (high):** the funnel should offer a repeat-revenue layer (membership, maintenance reminder, package/course, second rebook) after acquisition.
- **Capacity is part of ROI.** Median staff utilization was only 38% versus 80% at the 90th percentile.[S3] **Inference (medium-high):** ask about open chair/device hours before promising volume.

### Acquisition, retention, lead quality, and attendance

The operator’s actual scoreboard is:

`qualified inquiry → contacted → consult booked → deposit/confirmation → showed → treated → collected revenue → rebooked → retained/reactivated`

“More calls” obscures where money leaks. A call can be outside service area, price-only, contraindicated, wrong treatment, unreachable, unbooked, no-show, or one-and-done. **High-confidence inference** from Zenoti attendance data and operator complaints: PulpSense should report **cost per qualified booked consult, show rate, consult-to-treatment rate, collected revenue, and 90/180-day rebooking**, not CPL alone.

### Trust, regulation, and local reputation

Med spas sell elective medical care in a hospitality wrapper. Patients want “comfortable,” “confident,” “not rushed,” “not pressured,” while regulators/specialty guidance foreground licensing, training, sourcing, and oversight.[S7][S8][S9][S10] **High-confidence inference:** the winning local page must prove both halves—**clinical credibility + spa-level experience**.

## VOC language bank

### Operator vocabulary to mirror

Use: **booked consults, qualified patients, show rate, cancellation/no-show, rebook, second rebook, membership, treatment plan, chair/provider utilization, average ticket, collected revenue, new vs existing guests, reactivation, deposit, medical director, injector, before-and-after, reviews, local competition, attribution, ROI.**

Avoid leading with: **traffic, impressions, engagement, AI ads, leads, calls, omnichannel, visibility** unless immediately tied to the above scoreboard.

### Client vocabulary to show in proof

Use authentic themes: **“clean,” “professional,” “not pushy,” “no selling,” “took her time listening,” “explain everything,” “comfortable and confident,” “never felt rushed or pressured,” “licensed and trained.”**[S7][S8][S9]

## Buying triggers, objections, and proof requirements

**Likely triggers (inference; medium-high):** new location/provider/device with unused capacity; plateauing same-store growth; weak month after promotions stop; expensive no-shows; inconsistent front-desk follow-up; injector calendar gaps; lapsed database; competitor outranking/out-reviewing the practice; agency renewal or failed vendor; desire to launch memberships or a high-value treatment.

**Objections → required proof:**

- “We tried agencies/AI ads before.” → treatment-level source-to-revenue dashboard; anonymized live sample; ownership/access to ad accounts and data; 30/60/90-day plan.
- “Meta brings cheap/price shoppers.” → qualification logic, service-area gating, budget/timeline/interest questions, no discount-led creative, deposit/consult policy.
- “Leads don’t answer/show.” → response-time SLA, SMS/call/email cadence, booking handoff, reminders, deposit/reschedule/no-show recovery, show-rate benchmark.
- “Our front desk is already overloaded.” → who contacts leads, when, escalation rules, calendar integration, missed-call recovery.
- “Can you prove revenue?” → closed-loop collected-revenue attribution, not screenshots of CPL or booked calls.
- “Will this damage our medical brand?” → compliance review, provider credential proof, no guarantees/medical outcome claims, ethical review policy.
- “Our market is different.” → a live local treatment-intent/reputation/competitor audit, not national vanity benchmarks.

## Ad-to-funnel match

### Recommended promise hierarchy

1. **Outcome:** qualified consults that show and turn into treatment revenue.
2. **Economic mechanism:** diagnose local demand + fix response, booking, show, rebook, and reactivation leakage.
3. **Proof:** treatment-level attribution and operator dashboard.
4. **Diagnostic hook:** see which local competitors Google/ChatGPT/review surfaces recommend—and why.
5. **Only then:** a bounded call/consult target if backed by comparable evidence and defined terms.

### Required funnel sections

1. **Hero:** “More qualified consults that show—not another list of cheap leads.” CTA: **Get My Local Patient-Demand Audit**.
2. **Problem scoreboard:** visual leakage path from inquiry to retained patient.
3. **Local diagnostic preview:** competitor visibility, review themes, treatment-page gaps; label AI recommendations as a time-stamped snapshot.
4. **Mechanism:** attract → qualify → respond → book → confirm → recover → rebook/reactivate → attribute.
5. **Treatment economics:** injectable repeat path versus high-ticket device/course path.
6. **Clinical trust proof:** medical director, provider license/training, authorized products, consultation, protocols; client supplies and approves claims.
7. **Experience proof:** treatment-specific reviews and before/afters with consent, dates/context, and no outcome guarantee.
8. **Case study:** starting capacity, market, service, spend, qualified consults, show rate, treated patients, collected revenue, rebooks; define window and exclusions.
9. **Dashboard proof:** source → appointment → treatment → collected revenue; no vanity metrics.
10. **Fit / not fit:** established US med spa, available capacity, responsive team, compliant providers, adequate ticket/margin; not operators seeking instant Groupon-style volume.
11. **FAQ:** ownership, ad spend, lead handling, deposits, integrations, HIPAA/PHI boundaries, review ethics, cancellation.
12. **CTA + short application:** audit first, sales call second.

### Calculator inputs and outputs

**Inputs:** service line; average collected first-ticket; gross contribution margin (optional); consult-to-treatment rate; show rate; monthly qualified inquiries; existing response/booking rate; repeat visits/year or 12-month patient value; open provider/device slots; cancellation/no-show rate; ad spend.

**Outputs:** current vs improved **kept appointments**, first-treatment revenue, 12-month revenue, break-even qualified consults, revenue lost to no-shows, capacity required. Show ranges and let the operator change assumptions. Do **not** present projected revenue as guaranteed.

### Lead form / qualification questions

1. Role: owner/operator, medical director, practice manager, injector, marketing lead.
2. Location count and city/state/service radius.
3. Priority treatments (multi-select: toxin, filler, laser/energy, body contouring, skin, wellness/GLP-1, other).
4. Average collected ticket or range for priority treatment.
5. Open treatment capacity/week by provider/device.
6. Current monthly inquiries, consults booked, and approximate show rate.
7. Biggest leak: lead quality, response, booking, no-shows/cancels, consult conversion, rebooking/retention, reviews/local visibility, attribution.
8. Current channels and monthly ad-spend range.
9. Booking/CRM/POS system.
10. Do you use deposits/cancellation policy and automated reminders?
11. Medical director/provider licensure in place? (Yes / No / Need guidance—**not legal advice**.)
12. Timing and decision-makers for a 90-day pilot.

### CTA

Primary: **Get My Local Patient-Demand & Revenue-Leak Audit**
Microcopy: **See which treatments and competitors own local demand, where booked revenue is leaking, and the first 3 fixes—without committing to another lead-gen retainer.**

## Claims and angles to avoid

- **Unqualified “45 more calls in 90 days.”** Define qualified call, geography, treatment, baseline, media spend, tracking, and whether it booked/showed. Prefer qualified consults/kept appointments.
- **“Guaranteed patients/revenue/results”** or clinical outcome guarantees.
- **“ChatGPT recommends X” as permanent truth.** Recommendations are variable, personalized, and time-sensitive; show dated prompts/method and call it a snapshot.
- **Cheap lead/CPL hero messaging.** It attracts the same discount-volume anxiety operators already reject.
- **Unverified “#1/best/safest/board-certified” claims**, especially where credentials are nuanced.
- **Fake, purchased, gated, or sentiment-conditioned reviews; undisclosed staff/influencer testimonials; fake followers.** FTC’s rule directly addresses these.[S11]
- **Patient targeting or testimonials that imply sensitive health knowledge**, or uploading PHI to ad/AI systems without appropriate controls.
- **Revenue math that treats ASPS fees, tickets, bookings, or quoted package price as collected profit.**

## Messaging hypotheses to test

1. **Show-rate:** “Your problem may not be lead volume. Find the leak between inquiry, booked consult, and patient who actually shows.”
2. **Anti-agency:** “No black-box lead list: see every qualified consult, kept appointment, and collected dollar by treatment.”
3. **Anti-discount:** “Fill injectable and device capacity without becoming the cheapest med spa in town.”
4. **Retention:** “Turn one booked treatment into the second rebook—not another one-and-done promo patient.”
5. **Local competitive:** “See which med spas Google, reviews, and AI answers surface for your highest-value treatments—and the proof they have that you don’t.”
6. **Capacity:** “You already paid for the provider/device hours. Build qualified local demand around the capacity you need to fill.”
7. **Trust:** “Win the consult before the call with provider credentials, authorized-product confidence, treatment-specific reviews, and no-pressure education.”
8. **Reactivation:** “Your next revenue month may be sitting in your lapsed-patient list, not in a higher Meta budget.”
9. **Operator control:** “A 90-day patient-demand system your team can see and own—ads, follow-up, attendance, revenue, rebooking.”
10. **Audit-first CTA:** “Get a treatment-by-treatment local demand and revenue-leak audit before you hire another agency.”

## Evidence vs. inference / confidence notes

- **High confidence:** category scale, Zenoti operational benchmarks, FDA/FTC requirements, and ASPS procedure data are directly sourced. Zenoti is platform-specific and should be treated as directional, not a universal US census.
- **Medium confidence:** agency distrust, deal-site skepticism, and patient phrases are authentic search-indexed excerpts but not representative surveys. Use as copy-language candidates, then validate in 5–10 operator interviews/call transcripts.
- **Inference:** recommended funnel architecture, trigger list, qualification fields, calculator, and messaging tests are strategic translations of the evidence—not reported survey findings.
- **Proof gap before launch:** obtain 2–3 PulpSense med-spa or adjacent-practice case studies with raw denominator data (inquiries → booked → showed → treated → revenue → rebook), plus recorded buyer interviews. Do not manufacture “45 calls” support from industry averages.
