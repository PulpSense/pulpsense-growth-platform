Improving case acceptance in dentistry isn’t a sales problem. It’s almost always a clarity problem. When patients delay treatment, they're often telling us something important: they're unsure, overwhelmed, worried about cost, afraid of the unknown, or missing a clear next step.
Dental case acceptance
is a team workflow, not a single conversation. The doctor, hygienist, assistant, treatment coordinator, and front office team all play a role in creating one consistent patient experience. The goal is simple: make saying yes feel understandable, affordable, and safe.
What case acceptance means in dentistry
In a dental practice, case acceptance measures how often patients agree to proceed with recommended treatment. It is one of the clearest indicators of whether patients understand the value of care, trust the recommendation, and have a path forward that works for them.
But
case acceptance
is bigger than a percentage. It reflects how well your team communicates clinical findings, explains risk, verifies benefits, presents estimates, offers financing, and follows up when patients are not ready to schedule immediately.
How to calculate your dental case acceptance rate
Case acceptance rate = accepted treatment ÷ presented treatment × 100
Practices may track case acceptance by dollar amount, procedure count, provider, treatment coordinator, location, or treatment category. What matters most isn't how you track it — it's that you track it consistently.
For the
2026 Catalyst Index
, case acceptance was defined as "accepted treatment,” meaning treatment that's actually scheduled or completed. That distinction matters more than it might seem.
Most practice management systems (PMS) let teams designate treatment plan status, but that status alone can be misleading. A team member might mark treatment as accepted after a positive conversation, even if nothing was ever scheduled. A patient might say they plan to move forward, but are still unsure.
“Eligibility Pro has made me a hero to my insurance and treatment managers. Why? It saves us time and money and lets us spend more time with patients. And when we combined Eligibility Pro with AI, our treatment acceptance rate went up to 100%.”
-Tamara Whitley, Owner and Office Director, Whitley Family Dental
Counting only scheduled or completed treatment as accepted gives you a more honest picture. It connects the clinical recommendation to a real next step, which makes case acceptance a stronger signal for both patient experience and practice performance.
Here's why that matters beyond the number itself: case acceptance isn't just a front-desk or doctor metric. It reflects how consistently the whole team diagnoses, explains, estimates, schedules, and follows up on care. When that number is off, it's rarely a people problem. It's a process problem. And that's actually good news, because processes can be fixed.
What counts as a good case acceptance rate
A good case acceptance rate depends on your practice model, procedure mix, patient base, and how treatment is presented.

If you’re looking to improve case acceptance, start by asking a few questions:
Are more patients scheduling needed care?
Are fewer treatment plans sitting unscheduled?
Are estimates more accurate?
Are follow-ups happening on time?
Are patients hearing the same message from every team member?
The best
dental case acceptance metric
is one your team can understand, trust, and act on.
Why patients delay or decline treatment: cost, fear, urgency, and understanding
There are four major barriers I consistently see.
Confusion:
Patients don’t understand the problem, risk, or urgency
Fear:
Patients are worried about pain, outcomes, or the unknown
Cost uncertainty:
Patients are unsure what treatment will cost today and over time
Mixed Messages:
Different team members are inconsistent in their messaging and create doubt without meaning to.
When dental teams reduce friction in those areas,
case acceptance improves naturall
y. The conversation becomes less about convincing and more about guiding.
“When describing it to patients, I show them how AI validates my diagnosis. Someone not familiar with dental or radiographs can easily see where there are problems.”
-Evelyn Lahiji, COO, Children's Dental Fun Zone
How to improve case acceptance across the team
The most effective
treatment planning case acceptance strategies
are built around four principles:
Clarity:
Make the diagnosis and next steps easy to understand
Confidence:
Speak with calm certainty so patients can borrow your confidence
Consistency:
Every member of your team -- from clinicians to the front desk – is using the same messaging
Convenience:
Patients are given the tools, timing, and options to move forward
Patients need to understand what is happening, believe the recommendation, hear one aligned message, and have simple ways to move forward.
Present treatment plans in plain language and patient-centered benefits
Patients need a roadmap, not just a diagnosis. A treatment plan should help them understand what's happening, why it matters, what comes first, and what could happen if they wait.
Start with the concern, then connect it to the recommended solution. For example: “I’m concerned this tooth could break if we wait. The best way to protect it is with a crown.” That structure keeps the conversation clear:
Concern first
Solution second
Confidence throughout
Avoid leading with dental jargon or giving so many options the patient feels left to diagnose themselves. Provide your patients with calm, confident explanations of the next best steps.
Make the need visible with images, charting, and chairside education
Photos, radiographs, scans, periodontal charting, and visual treatment plans help turn an abstract problem into something concrete.
The key is showing the image, naming it, and connecting it to the next step. “This dark area is decay under the existing filling. That's why we’re recommending treatment now, before the tooth becomes painful or more difficult to restore.”
Chairside education
also gives patients something to revisit after the appointment. When practices can send treatment summaries, images, or educational materials by text or email, “I need to think about it” can become the start of a thoughtful follow-up conversation instead of a dead end.
“We’ve found our patient base and case acceptance rates growing because we’re using AI.We have these young associates that our patients are unfamiliar with, but because they’re using AI, their case acceptance rates are on par with mine.”
-Dr. Rick Hagstrom, Owner, A Shop For Smiles
Connect treatment planning to estimates and financing options
Clinical value and financial logistics are both important, but they shouldn’t be mixed together too early. The doctor’s role is to explain the health concern and recommended care. The administrative or treatment coordination team’s role is to help the patient understand how to make that care work.
That starts with
accurate benefits verification and clear estimates
. Patients should understand what insurance may contribute, what their estimated responsibility may be, and what options are available if they want to phase treatment or use financing. Automated insurance verification tools can eliminate hours spent calling payors and logging into portals with instant eligibility and benefits information for upcoming appointments written directly to coverage tables.
According to DentalPlans.com, the biggest concerns when it comes to affording dental care are:
Rising treatment costs 72%
Limited insurance coverage 50%
Emergency dental expenses 44%
Unpredictable or unclear pricing 36%
Lack of personal savings 14%
Supportive language matters, too. Instead of “Insurance doesn’t cover that,” try “Insurance may help with part of the cost. Let’s look at what works for you.” That small shift keeps the patient focused on solutions instead of barriers. Providing financial options, like membership plans or payment plans, will also make it easier for patients to accept treatment
When
clinical, front office, and financial systems work together
, teams can spend less time chasing information and more time helping patients move forward with confidence.
Follow up consistently on unscheduled treatment
The patient wasn’t ready to schedule their next step, but that doesn’t mean the treatment recommendation should just disappear after the appointment.
A consistent follow-up workflow
keeps needed care visible and gives patients another opportunity to ask questions, review costs, and schedule when they're ready.
Build a simple cadence for outreach. For example, send a same-day summary, follow up within a few days, and continue outreach based on urgency. Each message should be specific, helpful, and connected to the treatment discussed.
“The more information we can present to patients, especially visually, the more we can help them understand why they need the treatment that's been diagnosed. Ascend gives us that ability. I think it helps them to feel more confident in our diagnoses, and they accept treatment at a higher rate.”
-Amanda Bell, Practice Manager and Owner, Linrose Dental
A strong follow-up message might sound like this: “We wanted to check in about the crown we recommended for the upper right tooth. The goal is to protect it before it breaks or becomes painful. We can review your estimate, talk through financing, or help you find a time that works.”
Track case acceptance by provider, procedure, coordinator, and location
Once your workflow is in place, keep an eye out for patterns – both good and bad. That starts with tracking
overall case acceptance
, but you should also be reviewing by:
By provider:
Which clinicians are getting strong acceptance, and what communication habits can they share with the team?
By procedure:
Which treatment types are most often delayed or declined?
By coordinator:
Where are estimates, financing conversations, or follow-up workflows working well, and where are you seeing gaps?
By location:
For group practices and DSOs, which offices need additional support, training, or more consistent workflows?
Tools like analytics dashboards can help practices identify where treatment is being presented, accepted, and scheduled, helping leaders remove barriers, improve handoffs, and create a more consistent patient experience.
The Patient Is Ready. Is Your Team?
Treatment acceptance relies on every member of the team speaking the same language and understanding who is responsible for which part of the process in getting the patient to yes.
Because it’s not an admin issue.
It isn’t a hygiene issue.
It isn’t a doctor issue.
It’s a leadership issue.
When teams are aligned, trained, and supported by the right systems, “yes” stops being something you chase and starts being the next step in getting the patient back to better health!
Frequently Asked Questions
How do you improve case acceptance in dentistry?
Improve case acceptance in dentistry by making treatment easier to understand, easier to trust, and easier to schedule. Use plain language, show patients what you see with images or charting, provide accurate estimates, offer financing or phasing when appropriate, and follow up consistently on unscheduled treatment.
What should be included when presenting a dental treatment plan?
A dental treatment plan presentation should include the diagnosis, the reason treatment is recommended, the risk of waiting, the prioritized next step, the estimated cost, expected insurance contribution, payment or financing options, and a clear scheduling recommendation.
How can financing options improve treatment acceptance?
Financing options can improve treatment acceptance by reducing the immediate financial barrier for patients. When patients understand the value of care and have a manageable way to pay for it, they may feel more comfortable moving forward instead of delaying needed treatment.
Make saying yes feel simple, supported, and safe
Patients do not need more pressure. They need clarity. They need a team that speaks with calm confidence, uses the same language, explains benefits and estimates clearly, and follows up with care.
When your practice aligns treatment planning, benefits verification, financing, patient communication, and follow-up, case acceptance becomes less about closing a case and more about helping patients choose the care they already need.
